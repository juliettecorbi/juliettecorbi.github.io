Recent findings show that the stigma against radical right parties constitutes an obstacle to their electoral success, but that this stigma can decrease. Yet radical right actors are not merely passive targets of this stigma: they also deploy strategies to counter it. One such strategy is the use of femonationalist framings, in which they portray immigration as a threat to women's rights. This research note investigates the extent to which such framings succeed in destigmatising radical right parties by reducing their association with prejudice.

To analyse the effect of this strategy, I conducted an online survey experiment in France in 2024–2025 with approximately 7,000 respondents, focusing on the Rassemblement National (RN), a radical right party that is being normalised yet remains stigmatised, like most radical right parties in Western Europe. The analysis shows that the use of femonationalism by the Rassemblement National destigmatises the party among men, by increasing their perception that it supports women's rights. This effect, however, is not significant among women, whose security and rights are the ones invoked in these femonationalist framings. Overall, the findings show that the strategies radical right parties employ in order to be normalised are effective, but only among one part of the population.
